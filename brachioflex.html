Project · Hybrid Robotics

BrachioFlex

A brachiation robot with a flexible magnetic arm for inspecting the underside of steel structures. It combines a compliant continuum arm with magnetic adhesion, and uses reinforcement learning to autonomously generate swinging (brachiation) locomotion.

Overview

Inspecting where wheels and drones can't

Steel structures in factories, warehouses, bridges, and plants require regular inspection as they age, but working at height and in confined spaces is dangerous and labor-intensive. Conventional wheeled and crawler robots struggle to move along the underside of steel beams, and drones face limits on flight time and stability in tight spaces.

BrachioFlex takes inspiration from biological brachiation — the arm-over-arm swinging locomotion of primates — and pairs it with a flexible, magnetically adhering arm to move across steel ceilings and beam undersides that were previously hard to reach.

Research

How it works

Flexible magnetic-arm robot

The continuum arm embeds elastic elements to balance mobility with stiffness. Permanent magnets at the arm tips provide switchable adhesion to and release from steel surfaces. To switch adhesion reliably even during swinging, a dual-pulley mechanism was designed and prototyped with 3D printing.

Learning to swing

Brachiation motion is generated with reinforcement learning. Initial validation used a double-pendulum model, then extended to a full-body robot model with the flexible arm. Applying PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) optimized adhesion timing and arm-curvature control, achieving stable locomotion on inclines up to roughly 30°.

Outlook & publications

Where it's headed

BrachioFlex points toward new inspection technology for steel-structure undersides that conventional robots cannot reach — reducing labor, improving safety, and lowering maintenance costs for high or confined spaces. Combining compliant structures with reinforcement learning opens a path to next-generation inspection robots that adapt to diverse structural environments.

Part of this work was presented at the Construction Robot Symposium 2025 and published in the proceedings.
